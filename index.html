What is PUE and why is it important? +

PUE (Power Usage Effectiveness) is a metric that measures the efficiency of a data center's energy use. It's calculated by dividing the total facility power by the IT equipment power.

A perfect PUE would be 1.0, meaning all energy is used by computing equipment with no overhead. Most modern data centers have a PUE between 1.2 and 1.5. The lower the PUE, the more efficient the data center, resulting in lower operational costs and environmental impact.

How much does it cost to build a 1MW data center? +

The cost to build a 1MW data center can vary significantly based on location, tier level, and specific requirements. However, as a general estimate:

  • Tier I (basic): $10-13 million
  • Tier II (redundant components): $11-14 million
  • Tier III (concurrently maintainable): $18-20 million
  • Tier IV (fault tolerant): $20-22 million

These costs include building construction, power and cooling infrastructure, but exclude IT hardware costs. Use our calculator to get a more tailored estimate based on your specific requirements.

How can I optimize my data center costs? +

Several strategies can help optimize data center costs:

  • Improve energy efficiency: Lower your PUE by implementing efficient cooling solutions and power management.
  • Virtualization: Maximize server utilization through virtualization to reduce hardware requirements.
  • Right-sizing: Ensure your capacity matches your actual needs to avoid overprovisioning.
  • Consider colocation: For smaller deployments, colocation often provides better economics than building your own facility.
  • Extend refresh cycles: Carefully evaluate if hardware really needs replacement or can be maintained longer.
  • Automation: Reduce staffing costs through automation of routine tasks.
  • Location selection: Choose locations with favorable power costs and climate conditions.
What are the differences between data center tiers? +

Data center tiers, as defined by the Uptime Institute, represent different levels of data center reliability and availability:

  • Tier I (Basic): 99.671% uptime, no redundancy, single path for power and cooling.
  • Tier II (Redundant Components): 99.741% uptime, some redundant components but still single path distribution.
  • Tier III (Concurrently Maintainable): 99.982% uptime, multiple power and cooling paths but only one active, can perform maintenance without shutdown.
  • Tier IV (Fault Tolerant): 99.995% uptime, multiple active power and cooling paths, redundant components, compartmentalized to prevent single points of failure.

Higher tier levels provide greater reliability but also come with significantly higher costs for construction and operation.
